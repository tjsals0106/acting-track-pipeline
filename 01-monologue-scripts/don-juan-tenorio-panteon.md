# Don Juan Tenorio: Mi buen padre empleó en esto

- Character: Don Juan Tenorio
- Situation: Secretly back in Seville at night, he visits the pantheon his father built over the victims of his duels, and speaks to the statue of Doña Inés, the one love of his life.
- Word count: about 419
- Text source: scanned printing of *Don Juan Tenorio* (Galería Dramática, Madrid, 1905, no editor named), digitized on the Internet Archive (identifier donjuantenoriodr00zorr_0); OCR text with obvious scanning errors corrected. Original spelling.
- Rights: Zorrilla died in 1893, past the 70-year term in Korea; the 1905 printing names no editor and claims no editorial contribution. This is a source-based reading, not legal advice. No translation.
- Status: Backlog
- Source: José Zorrilla, *Don Juan Tenorio*, Parte segunda, Acto I, Escena III.

## What the speech does

The only scene where Don Juan drops the performance. The mockery ("buena sepultura os di") curdles into memory; by the time he addresses the marble he is speaking to a person, not a statue. The shiver at the end belongs to him, not to the audience.
