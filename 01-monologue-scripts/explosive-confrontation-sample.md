# Sample: Explosive Confrontation

Original practice monologue. Written for this track, not taken from any published play.

- Character: Renata, 30s, a litigator
- Situation: She is in her brother's office after finding out he has been hiding the family firm's losses for two years. She has just read the ledger he thought she would never see.
- Word count: about 160
- Range: high volume, high speed. The control breaks in stages, and the fury is aimed.

## Spine

Renata wants her brother to admit what he did in front of the people he lied to, and the obstacle is his instinct to manage her.

## Text

Don't. Don't you dare say "let me explain." You have had two years to explain, Marcus. Two years of quarterly calls and smiling at Dad and telling Aunt Lou the margins were fine.

I read the ledger. The real one. The one in the blue folder that you keep in the bottom drawer, which, by the way, was not locked, which tells me you wanted someone to find it, and it tells me you chose the wrong person.

Forty-one million. You moved it from the Lisbon account into the warehouse loan and you signed Dad's name to the transfer approval. Dad's name, Marcus. He can't hold a pen steady enough to sign a birthday card.

Sit down. No, stand, I don't care, you're not going to sit behind that desk and make this a meeting. This is a deposition, and I am the only one in the room who has read the exhibits.

I am going to walk into the board meeting on Monday. I am going to put the blue folder on the table. And you are going to be the one who explains it, in your own voice, with your own name on the page. Because if I do it, I will say everything. And I have been saving everything since we were eleven.

## Beat Analysis

### Beat 1: Don't. Don't you dare ... telling Aunt Lou the margins were fine
- Emotional state: furious, controlled by a thread
- Objective: to stop him from managing her
- Obstacle: he is already talking
- Tactic: command. Interrupt and list his offenses.
- Shift: the list gets faster, and that is where she loses the thread.

### Beat 2: I read the ledger ... you chose the wrong person
- Emotional state: cold, precise, almost delighted by the trap he set
- Objective: to show him she saw through the setup
- Obstacle: he thought the drawer would keep her out
- Tactic: expose. Turn his trick back on him.
- Shift: the sarcasm on "by the way" is the first real crack.

### Beat 3: Forty-one million ... birthday card
- Emotional state: incredulous, then furious at the forgery
- Objective: to make him feel the cost of using their father
- Obstacle: he does not think the father matters
- Tactic: wound. Use the most personal fact she has.
- Shift: "Dad's name" is the peak. Volume goes up.

### Beat 4: Sit down. No, stand ... read the exhibits
- Emotional state: commanding, reclaiming the room
- Objective: to take control of the space and the terms
- Obstacle: he wants to sit and perform calm
- Tactic: dictate. Give stage directions to the other person.
- Shift: she has the room. The rhythm slows for one beat, then speeds up again.

### Beat 5: I am going to walk into the board meeting ... since we were eleven
- Emotional state: fierce, with old hurt underneath
- Objective: to force a confession on her own terms
- Obstacle: he might refuse, and then she has to act alone
- Tactic: threaten, then reveal. The last line shows the history under the anger.
- Shift: the closing line lands low and quiet after the volume.

## Turn

"Dad's name, Marcus." The objective moves from exposing him to hurting him, and the tactic changes from expose to wound. The audience should feel her go too far, then stop herself.

## Physical and Vocal Choices

- Move through the room. Do not stand still until beat 4.
- Let the volume rise on "Dad's name" and drop immediately on "Sit down."
- Keep the consonants hard on the list in beat 1. Then soften the vowels in beat 5.
- Use one hard stop before "since we were eleven." Do not rush past it.
