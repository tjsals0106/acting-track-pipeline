# Sample: Vulnerable Breakdown

Original practice monologue. Written for this track, not taken from any published play.

- Character: Teo, 20s, a line cook who has just been fired for a mistake that was not entirely his
- Situation: He is alone in the walk-in fridge after closing, talking to himself, then to the one coworker who stayed behind.
- Word count: about 150
- Range: high vulnerability. The emotion breaks the sentences.

## Spine

Teo wants someone to tell him he is not a failure, and the obstacle is his fear that nobody will.

## Text

It was the sauce. Okay? It was the sauce, and I know what everyone is going to say, that I should have tasted it, that I should have checked the label. I did check the label. I checked it twice.

I keep counting things. Forty-two hotel pans. Six racks of onions. I counted them this morning and then again when I got here, and they were the same, and I was so relieved that I almost cried in front of the dish guy.

He asked me if I was okay. Nobody asks that here. They ask if the order is up.

My mom thinks I'm still at work. I haven't called her. If I call her, she's going to ask me what I'm going to do now, and I don't know. I don't know anything. I was going to get a real station by spring. I told her that. I told her in June.

Do you know what it's like when the cold gets into your hands and you can't tell if they're shaking or if it's just the cold? I stand here so I can feel something that makes sense.

I'm not okay. I'm not. You can say it. I need somebody to say it so I can stop pretending I didn't hear it.

## Beat Analysis

### Beat 1: It was the sauce ... I checked it twice
- Emotional state: defensive, rushed, close to tears
- Objective: to get his innocence on the record
- Obstacle: nobody is listening yet
- Tactic: plead. Repeat the claim to make it stick.
- Shift: "twice" is quieter than the rest. He is not sure anyone believes him.

### Beat 2: I keep counting things ... in front of the dish guy
- Emotional state: fragile, half-laughing at himself
- Objective: to explain the fear without naming it
- Obstacle: the explanation sounds ridiculous to him
- Tactic: confide. Share the odd detail as if it were a joke.
- Shift: the laugh stops at "relieved." The relief scares him.

### Beat 3: He asked me if I was okay ... order is up
- Emotional state: wondering, lonely
- Objective: to give weight to the one kind moment
- Obstacle: he cannot quite believe it happened
- Tactic: reflect. Hold up the kindness and examine it.
- Shift: he almost smiles, then does not.

### Beat 4: My mom thinks I'm still at work ... I told her in June
- Emotional state: shame, rising panic
- Objective: to avoid being asked about the future
- Obstacle: the future is the thing he most needs to talk about
- Tactic: deflect, then confess. He admits the lie to himself.
- Shift: "June" is the break. The sentence falls apart.

### Beat 5: Do you know what it's like ... makes sense
- Emotional state: physically shaking, searching for stillness
- Objective: to make the other person understand his body, not just his words
- Obstacle: the cold is the only thing he can name
- Tactic: show. Use the body as evidence.
- Shift: he stops talking and just stands there.

### Beat 6: I'm not okay ... I didn't hear it
- Emotional state: raw, direct, almost pleading
- Objective: to get one honest sentence out of the other person
- Obstacle: he is afraid of what the answer will be
- Tactic: demand. Ask directly for the words.
- Shift: the final line is a request, not a statement.

## Turn

"I told her in June." His objective shifts from defending himself to hiding, and the tactic fails. He cannot keep the lie going.

## Physical and Vocal Choices

- Keep moving or touching something until beat 5. Then stop.
- Let the breath go ragged after "June." Do not fix it.
- Use short phrases. Let some fragments trail off.
- Keep the voice low and close. The room is small.
