# La vida es sueño: Hipogrifo violento

- Character: Rosaura, a noblewoman disguised as a man
- Situation: Crossing the mountains into Poland at dusk on a runaway horse, she is thrown to the ground and curses the land that receives her so badly.
- Word count: about 110
- Text source: Project Gutenberg eBook #54436, https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/54436, a volunteer transcription of *Teatro selecto de Calderón de la Barca*, vol. 1 (Biblioteca de Autores Españoles, Madrid: Luis Navarro, 1881), edited by Juan Eugenio Hartzenbusch. Original spelling of that edition. Footnote markers and speaker labels removed.
- Rights: Calderón died in 1681; the editor Hartzenbusch died in 1880, so the edition is also past the 70-year term in Korea. The Gutenberg file is a typed copy of that printed text, with no added or modernized wording. This is a source-based reading, not legal advice. No translation.
- Status: Backlog

## Text

```text
Hipogrifo violento
Que corriste parejas con el viento,
¿Dónde rayo sin llama,
Pájaro sin matiz, pez sin escama,
Y bruto sin instinto
Natural, al confuso laberinto
Destas desnudas peñas
Te desbocas, arrastras y despeñas?
Quédate en este monte,
Donde tengan los brutos su Faetonte;
Que yo, sin más camino
Que el que me dan las leyes del destino.
Ciega y desesperada
Bajaré la aspereza enmarañada
Deste monte eminente,
Que arruga al sol el ceño de su frente.
Mal, Polonia, recibes
A un extranjero, pues con sangre escribes
Su entrada en tus arenas,
Y apénas llega, cuando llega á penas.
Bien mi suerte lo dice;
¿Mas dónde halló piedad un infelice?
```

## What the speech does

Rosaura arrives already wronged, and the speech is a single climb of images: beast, labyrinth, mountain, then the turn to Poland itself. Keep the rhetoric tight; the last question should land as genuine hurt, not declamation.
