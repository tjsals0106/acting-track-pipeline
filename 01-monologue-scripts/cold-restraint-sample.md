# Sample: Cold Restraint

Original practice monologue. Written for this track, not taken from any published play.

- Character: Miriam, 50s, a hospital administrator
- Situation: She is telling her adult son that she has already signed the papers to sell his childhood home. He has come to argue. She is calm and precise and does not want to be touched.
- Word count: about 120
- Range: low emotion, high control. The feeling is in the gaps.

## Spine

Miriam wants her son to accept the decision without a scene, and the obstacle is his need to be angry at her.

## Text

Sit down, Daniel. No, the chair by the window. I can see you from there.

I signed on Thursday. The agent was very kind about it. She said the market is good this season, which I believe is what people say when they have decided not to feel anything about a house.

You don't need to shout. I know what you want to say, and I have already heard it in the car, in the elevator, and in the kitchen you are standing in right now. You can keep the mug. The one with the chip. You always liked the chip.

The furnace is seventeen years old. Your father replaced the windows the year before he got sick, and he did not tell me until they were in. I found the receipts later. Forty-one of them, in a shoebox, under the stairs.

So I am not going to pretend this is a tragedy. It is a house. We are allowed to outgrow a house.

Please stop looking at me like that. I am being reasonable. It is the only thing I have left to do well.

## Beat Analysis

### Beat 1: Sit down ... the chair by the window
- Emotional state: controlled, slightly amused
- Objective: to place him where she can watch him
- Obstacle: he wants to stand and pace
- Tactic: direct. Give an instruction and wait for compliance.
- Shift: she gets what she asked for, and she knows it.

### Beat 2: I signed on Thursday ... not to feel anything about a house
- Emotional state: cool, with a thin edge of irony
- Objective: to show him the decision is final without arguing
- Obstacle: he will hear the news as a betrayal
- Tactic: dismiss. Quote the agent to make the sale sound ordinary.
- Shift: the irony slips a little and she hears herself.

### Beat 3: You don't need to shout ... You always liked the chip
- Emotional state: tender, held back
- Objective: to make him feel seen so he stops fighting
- Obstacle: the tenderness might break her
- Tactic: pre-empt. Name his anger before he speaks it.
- Shift: the mug line is the turn. She cannot keep her voice level on "the chip."

### Beat 4: The furnace is seventeen years old ... under the stairs
- Emotional state: grief, carefully stacked under facts
- Objective: to make him see what his father did without her saying it
- Obstacle: she refuses to be the one who cries
- Tactic: reveal. Give concrete facts, not feelings.
- Shift: the number forty-one lands. She has to stop.

### Beat 5: So I am not going to pretend ... only thing I have left to do well
- Emotional state: resolved, almost cold again
- Objective: to close the subject and end the conversation
- Obstacle: he is still looking at her
- Tactic: command. Restate the calm as a rule for both of them.
- Shift: she gets her control back, at a cost.

## Turn

"You always liked the chip." Her objective changes from managing him to reaching him, and the tactic fails. She has to reach for "reasonable" to recover.

## Physical and Vocal Choices

- Stand still. Hands folded or holding one object. No big gestures.
- Breathe on the commas. Hold the breath before "forty-one."
- Keep the pace even until "chip." Then slow down by half.
- Do not raise the volume at any point. The restraint is the performance.
